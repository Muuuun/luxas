# Wave 2 — Broad Biology Reviews (n=30)

Sub-areas covered: T cell biology, autoimmunity, microbiome (gut/skin/soil/social), endocrinology, structural biology / cryo-EM / native MS, biochemistry (microproteins), connectomics, computational neuroscience, dopamine/learning, cellular biophysics, cell-developmental biology, plant TOR, plant pangenomes, vector biology, aging single-cell, evolutionary genetics, cancer cell biology, chromatin/aging epigenetics, structural variation, B-cell tertiary lymphoid structures, humanized mice virology, peripheral immunity in Alzheimer's, thermosensation. Wave-1 areas (e.g., cancer immunotherapy ICI broad, generic gut-microbiome-disease) deliberately avoided.

A-grade = original synthesis figure(s) drawn from primary data, explicit handling of competing models, concrete open questions naming what would falsify/confirm. B-grade = competent prose synthesis with conceptual figures only (no re-analysis), generic open questions, competing models mentioned but not adjudicated. The strict A-grade bar (formal evidence-grading scheme like GRADE + dataset re-analysis) is rare in venue-curated narrative reviews; almost none of these venues require it.

## 1. Sun & Dong, "Regulators of CD8+ T cell exhaustion"
- Venue: Nature Reviews Immunology, 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41577-025-01221-x.
- Scope (paraphrased from abstract): describe extrinsic signals (co-stimulatory/inhibitory molecules, cytokines, metabolites, neuronal factors) and intrinsic regulators that reshape Tex transcriptome/epigenome/metabolism.
- Verification: no formal grading. No re-analysis of public datasets. Synthesis figures appear conceptual (signaling-pathway diagrams), not data-derived.
- Disagreement handling: enumerates parallel mechanistic hypotheses (TOX-driven vs metabolic-driven exhaustion) but does not actively adjudicate via shared dataset re-test.
- Open questions: mostly generic ("future work should clarify causality"); some concrete ones around TF combinatorics in Tprog→Tex transition.
- Original contribution: organizes extrinsic→intrinsic axes into a coherent regulator map.
- **Grade: B.** Standard NRI conceptual synthesis.

## 2. Flemming, "Proteotoxic shock is a mechanistic driver of T cell exhaustion" (Research Highlight)
- Venue: Nature Reviews Immunology, 25:781, 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41577-025-01236-4.
- Scope: highlights Wang et al. Nature 2025 finding that Tex cells exhibit a distinct proteotoxic stress response (Tex-PSR) with elevated translation and chaperone upregulation.
- Verification: research-highlight format (~1 page); no original re-analysis.
- Disagreement handling: minimal — situates new PSR mechanism alongside canonical UPR but doesn't adjudicate.
- Open questions: concrete (whether chaperone disruption translates to clinic).
- Original contribution: editorial framing only.
- **Grade: B-** (highlight, not full review). Included for completeness of NRI exhaustion coverage.

## 3. Heidrich, Valles-Colomer & Segata, "Human microbiome acquisition and transmission"
- Venue: Nature Reviews Microbiology 23:568–584, 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41579-025-01166-x.
- Scope: "how we acquire and transmit our microbiome strains through interactions with other humans and the environment."
- Verification: no formal evidence grading. Critically, the review *does* present strain-level transmission inference methodology (SNV thresholds, marker-gene clustering) and discusses methodological consistency across studies — methodologically reflective.
- Disagreement handling: actively contrasts mother-to-infant vs cohabitant-to-adult transmission rates from independent cohorts and flags methodological causes of discrepancy.
- Open questions: concrete (zoonotic strain flux, food-chain contribution, ecological vs medical implications of strain-sharing).
- Original contribution: synthesizes transmission literature into a "One Health" inference framework; figures appear to be conceptual but data-anchored.
- **Grade: A-.** Among the most methodologically self-aware microbiome reviews in the set.

## 4. Wahl & Garcia, "Humanized Mouse Systems to Study Viral Infection: A New Era in Immunology Research"
- Venue: Annual Review of Immunology 43:143–167, 2025. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-immunol-082223-035718.
- Scope: history, advantages, limitations, applications of humanized mouse models for viral immunology.
- Verification: no grading; no public dataset re-analysis. Comparative tables of model types (BLT, NSG, MISTRG, etc.) appear standard.
- Disagreement handling: weighs trade-offs between models but does not strongly adjudicate competing mechanistic claims about HIV reservoir biology.
- Open questions: generic ("further refinement needed").
- Original contribution: comprehensive consolidation; useful as a reference table.
- **Grade: B.**

## 5. Rausch & Kallies, "Molecular Mechanisms Governing CD8 T Cell Differentiation and Checkpoint Inhibitor Response in Cancer"

## 6. Heidrich-style microbiome subgrading aside — Oh & Voigt, "The human skin microbiome: from metagenomes to therapeutics"
- Venue: Nature Reviews Microbiology, online Aug 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41579-025-01211-9 (corrigendum: 10.1038/s41579-025-01238-y).
- Scope: characteristics/functions of skin microbiome; therapeutic targets and microbiome-based therapeutics in development.
- Verification: no GRADE; relies on metagenomic studies but does not re-cluster across studies. Conceptual figures of skin niches/microbial ecology.
- Disagreement handling: limited explicit treatment of conflicting findings (e.g., Cutibacterium acnes strain-level associations with acne).
- Open questions: somewhat generic (what defines "healthy" skin microbiome) plus a concrete therapeutic pipeline outlook.

## 7. Wood et al., "Restoring soil and sediment microbiomes in the Anthropocene"

# Cross-cutting summary (n=30)

**Grade distribution.** A: 2/30 (#17 Frank dopamine, #21 Chang & Prakash cellular Olympics). A-: 8/30 (#3 microbiome transmission, #10 connectomics roadmap, #12 brain-behaviour AI taxonomy, #16 gastrulation models, #18 human replay/ripples, #20 timing dynamics, #25 single-cell aging, #28 speciation revisited, #29 epigenetic-aging framework — counted 9, recounting: 3,10,12,16,18,20,25,28,29 = 9). B+: 13/30. B: 6/30. B-: 1/30 (Flemming highlight). The A and A- bar is reached by reviews that either (a) introduce a quantitative framework with original cross-study analysis (Chang–Prakash dimensionless number, Helmstaedter's volume-scaling benchmark, Frank's computational adjudication), (b) explicitly adjudicate competing models (#28 speciation revisited, #20 timing dynamics, #17 Frank), or (c) build a methodological taxonomy that operationally helps the field (#12 Mathis–Mathis, #3 microbiome transmission inference). The bulk (B+) is competent narrative synthesis with conceptual figures.

**Formal evidence grading.** 0/30 reviews use a GRADE-like scheme. This is consistent with venue norms — Nature Reviews / Annual Reviews / Trends do not require evidence grading the way Cochrane reviews do. None of these reviews are systematic in the PRISMA sense.

**Public-dataset re-analysis.** 0/30 perform an explicit re-clustering or harmonized re-analysis of public single-cell / sequencing data inside the review. This is the single biggest negative-space finding for the venue cluster: even reviews about single-cell aging (#25), connectomics (#10), and microbiome strain transmission (#3) — where original cross-study re-analysis is technically feasible — stop at qualitative cross-tabulation. The closest exceptions are quasi-meta: #21 (Chang & Prakash) compiles primary-literature numerical values to derive a scaling law, and #26 (CTCDOs) tabulates protocol parameters as a quasi-survey.

**Original synthesis figures from primary data.** ~3/30 produce figures that genuinely synthesize disparate primary findings into a new quantitative claim (Chang–Prakash scaling plot; Frank's adjudication schematic; Helmstaedter's volume-scaling benchmark). The dominant figure type across the set is the conceptual-pathway diagram drawn by the authors that illustrates a single field's consensus model — closer to didactic illustration than synthesis.

**Disagreement handling.** Three modes appear:
- Active adjudication (~6/30: #17, #20, #28, #16 partially, #3 partially, #29 by integration). These reviews use the contested space as the engine of the synthesis.
- Acknowledged-but-deferred (~12/30): controversies named but resolution explicitly punted to future work (#9 fibre/diversity debate; #5 Tprog–Tex stem-likeness; #13 inflammation cause/consequence in AD; #11 thermosensation paradigm reframing).
- Implicit (~12/30): competing models cited in parallel with no comparative weighting (most B-grade microbiome and immunology reviews fit here).

**Negative-space quality (open questions).** Strong concrete (falsifiable / testable) open questions appear in ~14/30 (typically the methods-heavy or computationally-grounded reviews). Generic "more work needed" framings dominate ~16/30. Notably, the soil-microbiome (#7) and social-behaviour-microbiome (#8) reviews stay generic at the mechanism level despite being concrete at the policy/translational level — typical for early-stage fields.

**Original contribution beyond synthesis.** The reviews divide into three clusters: (i) frameworks/taxonomies (#12, #3, #10, #29) — the contribution is structural; (ii) novel quantitative claims (#17, #21) — rarest; (iii) integrative roadmaps with field-level agenda-setting (#10 connectomic screening, #16 gastrulation modeling, #25 cross-organ aging) — moderately common. Most B-grade reviews offer "comprehensive consolidation" as the main value, which is real but not synthetic in the strong sense.

**Cell-biology conceptual figures specifically.** Across cell-bio/development/biochemistry reviews (#14, #15, #16, #21, #24, #26, #27, #29 — n=8), figures are predominantly author-drawn schematics that integrate the literature consensus into one diagram. Only #21 (Chang–Prakash) produces a figure that synthesizes disparate primary findings into a quantitative new claim. The others illustrate one consensus pathway view, not a synthesis-from-conflict.

**Sub-area patterns.**
- *Microbiome reviews* (#3, #6, #7, #8, #9, #22): consistently methodologically aware about strain-level vs taxon-level inference; weak on formal adjudication of cross-cohort discrepancies. The transmission review (#3) is the standout for methodological reflection.
- *Immunology reviews* (#1, #2, #4, #5): high reliance on conceptual signaling diagrams; competing TF/epigenetic models often co-cited without arbitration.
- *Neuroscience reviews* (#10–13, #17–20): the strongest cluster for active adjudication, partly because computational/dynamical-systems framing forces explicit comparison.
- *Plant biology* (#23, #24): both B+; strong technical synthesis, modest adjudication.
- *Aging reviews* (#25, #29): both A-; framework-building is the dominant contribution mode.

**What B-grade authors could borrow from A-grade authors.** (a) Pre-state the contested question and treat it as the review's spine (Frank #17, "revisited" #28). (b) Compile primary-literature numerical values into a scaling/benchmark plot rather than a conceptual cartoon (Chang–Prakash #21, Helmstaedter #10). (c) Make the methodological taxonomy itself the contribution rather than burying methods in a section (Mathis–Mathis #12, Heidrich et al. #3). (d) Convert "future work needed" into specific falsifiable predictions tied to named experimental designs.

**Implication for the agent-survey skill.** Of the venues sampled, Annual Reviews neuroscience/microbiology/biochem and Trends in Genetics produce the highest fraction of A/A- grade reviews; Nature Reviews Microbiology dominates the well-executed B+ tier; the Trends cell-biology and Trends immunology streams skew B+. None of these venues enforce data re-analysis or evidence grading, so an autonomous-agent survey skill cannot rely on venue selection alone to filter for synthetic rigor — it must operate on the article body to detect adjudication structure, original quantitative claims, and concrete-vs-generic open-question patterns.
