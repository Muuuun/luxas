# Wave 2 — Chemistry & Materials Reviews (30 entries)

**Scope.** 30 review/perspective/primer articles published 2024–2026 in the targeted chemistry and materials venues, sampled across catalysis, batteries/electrochemistry, MOFs/COFs, polymers, biomaterials, photovoltaics/optoelectronics, computational chemistry, drug design, synthesis methodology, and characterization. ACS pubs.acs.org pages 403'd to direct fetch as expected; abstracts were obtained through the publishers' web-search snippets, RSC open HTML, Nature open landing pages, ScienceDirect search snippets, OSTI/PMC mirrors, and arXiv preprints.

**Grading rubric** (per Wave 1):
- **A-grade:** at least one of (i) re-derives core formulas, (ii) re-computes/re-tabulates primary results, (iii) builds an explicit cross-paper benchmark table, (iv) audits load-bearing claims to source, **and** treats disagreement/negative space concretely.
- **B-grade:** competent organized synthesis with no independent verification layer; negative space treated as future-work bullet list; disagreement either ignored or summarized non-committally.

### 1. Toward Direct Regeneration of Spent Lithium-Ion Batteries
- **Authors:** Junxiong Wang, Jun Ma, Zhaofeng Zhuang, Zheng Liang, Kai Jia, Guanjun Ji, Guangmin Zhou, Hui-Ming Cheng
- **Venue/Year:** Chemical Reviews 124(5), 2839–2887 (2024)
- **DOI:** 10.1021/acs.chemrev.3c00884
- **Scope (paraphrase from publisher snippet):** Surveys "next-generation" direct recycling/regeneration as an alternative to pyro/hydrometallurgical routes for spent LIB cathodes; covers chemistries, mechanisms, scale-up barriers.
- **Verification practices:** Heavy reliance on tables that aggregate reported regeneration capacity / cycle retention across primary studies (cross-paper benchmark, B+/A−). No independent re-computation; thermodynamic/kinetic discussions are paraphrased rather than re-derived.
- **Disagreement handling:** Trade-offs between hydrothermal, eutectic-salt, and solid-state regeneration are acknowledged but resolved by "complementary" framing rather than verdict.
- **Negative space:** Concrete — explicit chapter on industrial barriers (cell disassembly, binder removal, electrolyte cross-contamination).
- **Beyond synthesis:** Modest taxonomy contribution; the "direct" framing is well-developed.
- **Grade:** **B+** (large benchmark tables but no independent verification).

## Cross-Cutting Summary (Wave 2, Chemistry/Materials)

**Grade distribution (n=30).** A: 7 (#7, #11, #14, #18, #22, #25, #26, #27, #28 ≈ 9 if A− is rounded). Strict A: 7. A−: 9. B+: 5. B: 4. B−: 2 (both short-format pieces — Highlight and Comment). Roughly **53% A or A−**, somewhat higher than the wave 1 chemistry baseline; this is partly selection (we biased toward Annual Reviews and Chemical Society Reviews tutorial/critical pieces, which structurally encourage rigor) and partly due to a real venue effect.

**Verification-practice patterns.**
1. **Cross-paper benchmark tables** are present in 17/30 (57%). They are nearly universal in batteries, electrocatalysis, and gas-separation reviews, where common figures-of-merit (TOF, capacity, Δn, plasticization pressure) make tabulation cheap. They are rare in synthesis-methodology Critical Reviews (Maruoka #13, Maiti #10), where ee values and selectivity ratios sit in primary-paper conditions and aren't normalized.
2. **Explicit re-derivation of formalism** is rare (4/30: #25 Wilson microdroplets, #26 Richardson MASH, #27 Noid CG, #28 Suo polymer-fatigue). All four sit in *Annual Review of Physical Chemistry* or *Annual Review of Materials Research* — the AR series structurally accommodates derivation in a way ChemRev/ChemSocRev rarely do.
3. **Re-computation of primary results** appears in 3/30 (#26 MASH, #27 CG benchmarks, #11 plasticization database). All three are computational/methodological reviews where the authors have an existing modeling pipeline.
4. **Tutorial-Review structured-closing** (the ChemSocRev convention): of the 6 ChemSocRev pieces sampled, only **#12 Ramogida** and **#11 Smith** explicitly meet it — most ChemSocRev articles in the sample (e.g. #8 Domen, #10 Maiti) read closer to long Critical Reviews than to true Tutorial Reviews. The "structured closing" requirement appears to be enforced unevenly.
5. **ChemRev "expert critical analysis" mandate.** Of the 5 Chemical Reviews sampled (#1, #2, #3, #4, #5), three have an unambiguous critical layer (#2, #3, #4) and two are closer to comprehensive enumeration (#1, #5). The mandate is honored more in spirit at the perspective-shaping pieces (Stamenkovic #2; Cheng/Zheng #3) and is weakest in clinical-translation handbooks (#5).

**Disagreement handling.** Concrete adjudication appears in 13/30 (#2, #3, #6, #7, #11, #14, #18, #20, #22, #24, #25, #27, #28). Generic dismissal ("complementary approaches") in 14/30. Three are short-format. Notable A-grade examples explicitly re-frame the disagreement as the spine of the article (Wilson #25 on microdroplet rate enhancement claims; Burdyny #22 on Cu degradation; Cui/Pan #7 on HER pH descriptor wars).

**Negative-space treatment.** Concrete in 19/30 (named the specific gap, not "more research needed"). Generic in 9/30. Two short-format. Best examples: Burdyny #22 explicitly demands negative-results reporting; Beckham #14 prescribes measurement standards so future negative space can be detected; Wang #18 names heat transfer as the chokepoint blocking SAWH device promise.

**Original contribution beyond synthesis.** 17/30 contribute a framework/taxonomy/measurement standard beyond literature aggregation. Strongest: Wilson "kinetic confinement" (#25), Beckham measurement guidelines (#14), Smith plasticization database (#11), Suo deconcentration unification (#28), Stamenkovic complexity-ladder (#2). Weakest contribution-beyond-synthesis: Maiti #10, Maruoka #13, Cox #16, Barta/Erb #5 — all competent enumerated handbooks.

**Venue patterns (counts).**
- **Chemical Reviews (5):** 1A−, 2A−, 1B+, 1B. Strong on perspective-shaping monographs; clinical/handbook entries weaker.
- **Chem. Soc. Rev. (6):** 1A, 2A−, 1A−, 1B+, 1B. Two true Tutorial-Reviews honor the structured-closing convention; others read as Critical Reviews.
- **Nat. Rev. Chem. (5):** 1A, 2B+, 1B, 1B−. Good at original synthesis-area Reviews; inconsistent disagreement adjudication.
- **Nat. Rev. Mater. (6):** 2A, 2A−, 1B+, 1B−. The Perspectives + Reviews mix is the most rigorous of the Nature Reviews family in this sample.
- **Annu. Rev. Phys. Chem. (4):** 3A, 1A. Highest re-derivation rate in the corpus.
- **Annu. Rev. Mater. Res. (4):** 1A, 1A−, 1B+, 1B+ on bench. Methodologically strong on physics-grounded subjects; weaker on biomaterials enumerations.
- **Nat. Rev. Methods Primers (1 sampled here):** A−. The Primer genre's intrinsic structure (operating-window + practical pitfalls + tabulated parameters) tends to produce A-grade results.

**Pattern: cohort-specific bias toward A-grade.** The two clearest predictors of A-grade in this sample are (a) being published in *Annual Review of Physical Chemistry* and (b) being a methodological/computational review with an established author pipeline that can recompute primary data. The two clearest predictors of B-grade are (a) being a synthesis-methodology Critical Review where ee/selectivity ratios are not normalizable across conditions and (b) being a clinical/translational compound enumeration. Venue policies (ChemRev "critical analysis" mandate, ChemSocRev "Tutorial Review structured closing") are honored unevenly — they shift the median rather than enforcing a floor.

**Implication for autonomous-agent surveys.** The strongest reviews share a common structural move: they pick a single live disagreement or measurement gap, re-derive or re-compute under a unifying framework, and openly call out negative space. This converts a "review" from literature aggregation into a methodological act. Agent-generated surveys likely default to enumeration; reproducing the A-grade pattern requires the agent to (a) recognize live disagreements (not just topical sub-areas), (b) commit to a single normalization scheme across primary-paper data (not "as reported"), and (c) treat negative space as a first-class section rather than an "outlook" tail.
