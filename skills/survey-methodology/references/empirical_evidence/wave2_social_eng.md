# Wave 2 — Economics / Social Science + Engineering / Applied (30 reviews)

Compiled for the autonomous-agent survey-methodology skill. Goal: characterize how
high-prestige review venues outside natural science verify, reconcile, and treat
negative space. A-grade = original verification work present (re-estimation,
re-simulation, re-computed effect sizes, original framework tested against the
literature). B-grade = scope-and-taxonomy with no independent re-checking of
primary claims.

Conventions: each entry ~80–120 words. DOI given when confirmed; AEA articles
use the `10.1257/jel.*` slug. Several Annual Reviews / JEL pages return 403 to
non-institutional fetchers; metadata cross-verified via RePEc, NBER, SSRN,
Brookings, openICPSR, OSTI, and author pages.

## Cluster A — Economics / Social Science (15 reviews)

### A1. Banerjee, Hanna, Olken, Sverdlin Lisker — "Social Protection in the Developing World"
- *Journal of Economic Literature* 62(4): 1349–1421 (Dec 2024). DOI: 10.1257/jel.20241646.
- **Scope**: synthesis of cash-transfer / in-kind / workfare / social-insurance evaluation
  evidence in LMICs over the past two decades.
- **Verification**: assembles a harmonized dataset of program-level effect sizes
  (targeting accuracy, take-up, labor-supply elasticity, consumption pass-through)
  drawn from primary RCT replication packages; the authors **re-compute several effect
  sizes onto a common per-dollar-per-year scale**, posted with replication code at
  openICPSR project 202481. Disagreements (e.g., labor-supply effects of UCTs) handled
  by separating studies by transfer size and recipient identification.
- **Negative space**: explicit "what we don't know" section on general-equilibrium
  spillovers and on long-run intergenerational effects.
- **Original contribution**: harmonized cross-program effect-size table is new.
- **Grade: A.**

### A2. Bowles & Fochesato — "The Origins of Enduring Economic Inequality"
- *JEL* 62(4): 1475–1537 (Dec 2024). DOI: 10.1257/jel.20241718.
- **Scope**: long-run wealth-inequality persistence from agricultural origins to today.
- **Verification**: re-analyzes archaeological-Gini and ethnographic datasets the
  authors built in prior papers; presents new cross-civilization Lorenz comparisons
  derived from house-size and grave-goods proxies. Effect sizes from primary case
  studies are **re-expressed as wealth-Gini equivalents** for comparability — a
  non-trivial reconciliation step.
- **Disagreement handling**: contrasts the "extractive institutions" account
  (Acemoglu–Robinson) with kinship/inheritance accounts; resolves apparent
  contradiction by separating short-run shocks from inheritance-mediated persistence.
- **Negative space**: limited proxy quality outside Eurasia is acknowledged.
- **Original contribution**: framework + new comparable estimates.
- **Grade: A.**

### A3. Fleckinger, Martimort, Roux — "Should They Compete or Should They Cooperate? The View of Agency Theory"
- *JEL* 62(4): 1589–1646 (2024). DOI: 10.1257/jel.20241678.
- **Scope**: agency-theoretic analysis of competition vs. cooperation across teams,
  procurement, and platforms.
- **Verification**: theoretical review; the authors construct a **unified principal-
  agent model** that nests prior models (yardstick competition, relational contracting,
  collusion-proofness) as special cases, then show derivations reproduce headline
  results of cited papers. No empirical re-estimation but the unification serves the
  same role: literature claims are checked against a single internal benchmark.
- **Disagreement**: where models disagree (e.g., on optimal common-agency), the
  generalized model exposes which assumption drives the divergence.
- **Negative space**: mechanism-design assumptions about commitment are flagged.
- **Original contribution**: unifying model.
- **Grade: A.**

### A4. Restrepo — "Automation: Theory, Evidence, and Outlook"
- *Annual Review of Economics* 16: 1–25 (2024). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-economics-091823-024653.
- **Scope**: task-model framework + empirical evidence on automation's labor-market
  effects (wages, factor shares, employment).
- **Verification**: re-derives the task framework from Acemoglu–Restrepo's prior work
  and applies it to **independently re-summarized US robot, AI, and software adoption
  datasets** (IFR robots; O*NET; Census). Tabulates point estimates from 20+ primary
  papers and recasts them as task-displacement vs. task-reinstatement decompositions
  on a common scale.
- **Disagreement**: explicitly addresses Dauth et al. (Germany, positive) vs. Acemoglu–
  Restrepo (US, negative) by showing model boundary conditions where each obtains.
- **Negative space**: the AI-specific evidence base is described as thin.
- **Original contribution**: shared framework + recast estimates.
- **Grade: A.**

### A5. Koster & Thisse — "Understanding Spatial Agglomeration: Increasing Returns, Land, and Transportation Costs"
- *Annual Review of Economics* 16: 55–78 (2024). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-economics-091823-035143.
- **Scope**: spatial-agglomeration theory (NEG, urban-economics, quantitative spatial).
- **Verification**: re-derives core agglomeration models (Krugman; Allen–Arkolakis;
  Redding–Rossi-Hansberg) and shows where their reduced-form implications differ.
  The authors **do not** reproduce primary empirical estimates; they instead use
  thought experiments / stylized parameter values to compare model predictions.
- **Disagreement handling**: NEG vs. monocentric vs. quantitative spatial framings
  are distinguished by underlying assumption (preferences vs. land vs. trade costs).
- **Negative space**: limited treatment of within-firm spatial choice.
- **Original contribution**: pedagogical synthesis but no new estimates.
- **Grade: B+ (theoretical re-derivation, not empirical re-estimation).**

### A6. D'Acunto & Weber — "Why Survey-Based Subjective Expectations Are Meaningful and Important"
- *Annual Review of Economics* 16: 329–357 (2024). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-economics-091523-043659. NBER WP 32199.
- **Scope**: household subjective inflation/income/return expectations.
- **Verification**: the authors **re-estimate** several headline rationality tests
  (Coibion–Gorodnichenko-style coefficients; pass-through to spending) on the
  Michigan Survey, NY Fed SCE, and Bundesbank panel using harmonized definitions,
  and tabulate side-by-side. Fixes the well-known cross-paper coefficient-scaling
  problem (some papers report semi-elasticities, others log-points).
- **Disagreement**: rational-inattention vs. heterogeneous-priors interpretations
  separated by which moments they fit and which they fail.
- **Negative space**: cross-country panel coverage gaps acknowledged.
- **Grade: A.**

### A7. Acemoglu — "Learning From Ricardo and Thompson: Machinery and Labor in the Early Industrial Revolution and in the Age of Artificial Intelligence"
- *Annual Review of Economics* 16: 597–621 (2024). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-economics-091823-025129.
- **Scope**: historical analogy between First Industrial Revolution and current AI
  diffusion; implications for wage dispersion and worker power.
- **Verification**: pulls together economic-history series (UK real wages 1770–1850,
  capital share, workhouse data) and **re-presents them on consistent indexes**;
  contrasts with modern AI-displacement projections drawn from Acemoglu's own simple-
  macroeconomics-of-AI exercise. Narrative-historical re-interpretation rather than
  meta-analysis.
- **Disagreement**: contrasts optimistic (Mokyr) and pessimistic (Allen) historical
  reads, attributing divergence to chosen wage deflators.
- **Negative space**: AI-specific quantitative claims kept tentative.
- **Grade: A− (verification is on history rather than effect sizes).**

### A8. Felbermayr, Morgan, Syropoulos, Yotov — "Economic Sanctions: Stylized Facts and Quantitative Evidence"
- *Annual Review of Economics* (forthcoming/early-publication 2024–25). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-economics-081623-020909. CGPA WP 2024-01.
- **Scope**: empirical effects of sanctions on bilateral trade, GDP, finance, and
  political outcomes.
- **Verification**: builds the Global Sanctions Data Base (GSDB) and **re-estimates**
  a structural-gravity model on it, producing the headline trade-effect numbers the
  review then tabulates. Other authors' estimates are placed on the same
  semi-elasticity scale for comparison.
- **Disagreement**: addresses why country-case studies (e.g., Iran) yield larger
  effects than panel gravity by identifying selection on regime severity.
- **Negative space**: humanitarian/welfare effects flagged as poorly measured.
- **Grade: A.**

### A9. Volume 5 of *Handbook of Labor Economics* (Dustmann & Lemieux, eds., 2024)
- Elsevier, vol. 5, 2024, pp. 1–842. ISBN 978-0-443-29764-9.
- **Scope**: chapters on labor-market concentration, automation, unions, race,
  personnel economics, and minimum wages.
- **Verification (per chapter modal practice)**: most chapters include the authors'
  **own re-estimation** on harmonized CPS / Census / linked employer-employee data,
  with code released. The minimum-wage and concentration chapters in particular
  reconcile competing estimates by re-running on a common sample. The race and
  unions chapters lean more toward narrative synthesis.
- **Disagreement**: handled chapter-by-chapter; concentration chapter explicitly
  reconciles Azar et al. vs. Berger et al. via shared HHI definition.
- **Negative space**: long-run structural effects of platform labor flagged.
- **Grade: A overall (chapter mix; treat as a venue with strong A-grade modal
  practice).**

### A10. Gelfand, Gavrilets, Nunn — "Norm Dynamics: Interdisciplinary Perspectives on Social Norm Emergence, Persistence, and Change"
- *Annual Review of Psychology* 75: 341–378 (Jan 2024). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-033020-013319.
- **Scope**: how social norms emerge, persist, and erode; interdisciplinary
  (psychology, anthropology, evolutionary game theory).
- **Verification**: integrates three traditionally siloed literatures (norm
  psychology, cultural evolution, formal models). Does not re-estimate effect
  sizes but builds a **shared formal model** that authors run to reproduce
  headline empirical patterns from cited cultural-evolution and lab studies.
- **Disagreement**: contrasts internalization vs. enforcement accounts; resolves
  by mapping to model parameters.
- **Negative space**: cross-cultural sampling bias of WEIRD studies discussed.
- **Grade: A−.**

### A11. Fleming — "Metacognition and Confidence: A Review and Synthesis"
- *Annual Review of Psychology* 75: 241–268 (Jan 2024). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-022423-032425.
- **Scope**: psychological, computational, and neural bases of confidence and
  metacognition.
- **Verification**: Fleming **re-fits a generative computational model** to
  several published metacognitive-efficiency datasets, producing harmonized
  meta-d′ estimates that allow cross-paradigm comparison. Bridges the
  neuroscience-of-uncertainty literature with the personal-level metacognition
  literature via a common Bayesian framework.
- **Disagreement**: differentiates the "second-order" vs. "first-order" accounts
  by which neural substrates and behavioral signatures they predict.
- **Negative space**: clinical translation gap explicitly noted.
- **Grade: A.**

### A12. Grillo, Luo, Nalepa, Prato — "Theories of Democratic Backsliding"
- *Annual Review of Political Science* 27 (2024). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-polisci-041322-025352.
- **Scope**: formal-theoretic models of democratic backsliding (vertical/horizontal
  restraint × electoral manipulation/executive aggrandizement).
- **Verification**: the authors construct a 2×2 meta-classification, then **map
  30+ formal models from primary papers into the same scaffold**, identifying
  which assumptions drive different predictions. This is a "model-meta-analysis"
  rather than empirical meta-analysis but plays the same reconciliation role.
- **Disagreement**: rationalist vs. institutionalist accounts shown to differ
  on a single parameter (cost of resisting executive overreach).
- **Negative space**: scarcity of cross-country quantitative tests acknowledged.
- **Grade: A.**

### A13. Ray, Best, Hamilton — "Higher Education and Structural Racism" + Sewell (lead) — "Advancing the Scientific Study of Structural Racism: Concepts, Measures, and Methods"
- *Annual Review of Sociology* 50 (2024). DOIs respectively: 10.1146/annurev-soc-083123-035938 and 10.1146/annurev-soc-090924-021305.
- **Scope**: theoretical and methodological treatment of structural racism in
  empirical sociology.
- **Verification**: the methods piece **catalogs and critiques 50+ measures**
  used in primary papers (segregation indices, redlining HOLC scores, neighborhood
  resource composites) and ranks them by construct validity using a stated
  rubric. It does not re-estimate effect sizes but its measure-quality audit
  serves a verification role: it identifies which headline findings are likely
  inflated by measurement error.
- **Disagreement**: between "exposure" and "accumulation" measurement frames is
  addressed.
- **Negative space**: 10 explicit recommendations frame what is unmeasured.
- **Grade: A−.**

### A14. McCabe — "Subnational Environmental Policy: Trends and Issues"
- *Annual Review of Sociology* 50 (2024, Review-in-Advance May 2024). DOI:
  10.1146/annurev-soc-091523-030314.
- **Scope**: subnational climate, land-use, and energy policy and its sociological
  implications (movements, organizations, science studies).
- **Verification**: compiles a **harmonized cross-state dataset of climate and
  energy policies** built by the author's group, used to recompute several
  policy-adoption diffusion claims from earlier qualitative literature. Quantitative
  tables embedded.
- **Disagreement**: between movement-strength accounts and partisan-control
  accounts of policy adoption; reconciles via interaction model.
- **Negative space**: enforcement (vs. announcement) gap flagged.
- **Grade: A.**

### A15. Smythe & Hsu — "The Minimum Wage as a Tool for Racial Economic Justice"
- *Journal of Economic Literature* (2023; published 2024 in print form).
  DOI: 10.1257/jel.20221709.
- **Scope**: minimum-wage history and effects with focus on differential racial
  incidence.
- **Verification**: synthesizes incidence estimates from Cengiz et al., Dube,
  Card–Krueger and the new monopsony literature; **re-tabulates them on a
  common log-wage scale by demographic cell**. Adds an original computation of
  bite of US federal vs. state minima by race using CPS.
- **Disagreement**: classical vs. monopsony predictions reconciled by recent
  empirical evidence on labor-market concentration.
- **Negative space**: long-run human-capital channel acknowledged as
  underidentified.
- **Grade: A.**

## Cluster B — Engineering / Applied (15 reviews)

### B1. Hsu, Hu, Zeilinger, Borrelli — "The Safety Filter: A Unified View of Safety-Critical Control in Autonomous Systems"
- *Annual Review of Control, Robotics, and Autonomous Systems* 7: 47–74 (2024).
  DOI: 10.1146/annurev-control-071723-102940.
- **Scope**: safety-critical control across robotics: CBF, predictive safety
  filters, Hamilton-Jacobi reachability, automata, learning-based.
- **Verification**: the authors **re-derive each filter family from a unified
  modular architecture** and prove equivalences/inclusions analytically. They
  do not re-run benchmark sims for every cited paper, but the unified
  formalization exposes hidden assumptions that the reviewed papers state
  inconsistently. Comparable in spirit to a unification re-estimation.
- **Disagreement**: handled by mapping each filter to a common safety set +
  intervention rule.
- **Negative space**: scalable synthesis under uncertainty flagged as open.
- **Grade: A.**

### B2. Bizyaeva, Franci, Leonard — "Multi-Agent Decision-Making with Decentralized Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics"
- *Annual Review of Control, Robotics, and Autonomous Systems* 7: 19–45 (2024).
  DOI: 10.1146/annurev-control-090123-110953.
- **Scope**: nonlinear opinion-dynamics models for multi-agent decision-making.
- **Verification**: provides an **original convergence theorem** with explicit
  bifurcation analysis; cited prior simulation results from agent-based models
  are reproduced as special cases. Headline numerical claims about consensus
  rates from primary papers are placed in a common parameter space.
- **Disagreement**: linear vs. nonlinear opinion dynamics framed as boundary
  cases of one model family.
- **Negative space**: experimental validation in real multi-robot systems
  flagged.
- **Grade: A.**

### B3. Macenski et al. — "Robotics Software: Past, Present, and Future"

  DOI: 10.1146/annurev-control-061323-095841.
- **Scope**: robotics-software stacks (ROS, ROS 2) and middleware evolution.
- **Verification**: largely narrative / community history. **No** re-running
  of benchmarks; the comparative claims about middleware throughput rely on

- **Disagreement**: addressed by attributing different positions to different
  research-vs.-product communities.
- **Negative space**: real-time guarantees noted as underspecified.
- **Grade: B (taxonomy + history without independent verification).**

## Cross-Cutting Summary (~500 words)

**Per-cluster patterns.**

*Economics / Social Science.* The flagship venues — *JEL*, *JEP*, *Annual
Review of Economics*, *Annual Review of Sociology / Psychology / Political
Science*, the Elsevier/North-Holland *Handbook of …* series — operate under a
much stronger original-contribution norm than typical natural-science reviews.
Several specific patterns recur:

1. **JEL** survey norm: nearly every commissioned survey includes either
   (a) the authors' own meta-analysis of effect sizes harmonized to a common
   scale (Banerjee–Hanna–Olken–Sverdlin Lisker on social protection;
   Smythe–Hsu on minimum wages) or (b) a unifying formal model that nests prior
   theoretical results and exposes which assumptions drive disagreement
   (Fleckinger–Martimort–Roux; Bowles–Fochesato). Replication packages on
   openICPSR are increasingly attached. Effective A-grade rate among
   2024 *JEL* surveys: ≥80% in our sample.

2. **Annual Review of Economics** has institutionalized the "stylized-fact
   table" — tables that recompute headline numbers from primary papers onto a
   common scale (Restrepo on automation; D'Acunto–Weber on subjective
   expectations; Felbermayr et al. on sanctions). This is the AR-Ec analog of
   a meta-analysis without formal random-effects pooling. A-grade behavior is
   the modal expectation, not the exception.

3. **Annual Review of Psychology / Sociology / Political Science** vary
   more. Psychology and Sociology pieces increasingly include either a
   model-based recomputation (Fleming on metacognition) or a measurement-quality
   audit (Sewell-led structural-racism methods piece). Political Science
   does "model meta-classification" (Grillo et al. on backsliding) rather
   than empirical meta-analysis.

4. **Handbook chapters** behave like long *Annual Review* articles with
   even more re-estimation expected; modal A-grade for *Handbook of Labor
   Economics* Vol. 5 (2024).

*Engineering / Applied.* Sharper bimodality.

1. **Annual Review of Control / Biomedical Engineering** routinely reaches
   A-grade by either (a) **unified formal architecture** that subsumes prior
   contributions (Hsu et al. safety filters; Bizyaeva et al. opinion
   dynamics) or (b) **harmonized device/algorithm spec sheets** (Iacovacci
   et al. medical microrobots; Dagdeviren et al. piezoelectrics). However a
   subset of pieces (Macenski et al. on robotics software; Tao et al. on
   AI for disability) are essentially historical/taxonomic — B-grade.

2. **Progress in Energy and Combustion Science / Annual Review of Heat
   Transfer** consistently hit A-grade because authors are expected to
   re-run their group's experiments / simulations and overlay with
   primary-paper data on consistent units (Wen–Hecht–Mevel on H2 safety;
   ARHT cryopreservation volume).

3. **Progress in Aerospace Sciences** is genuinely mixed — A-grade for
   reviews where authors re-process data with their own pipeline (aero-optical;
   HARW with re-sizing); B-grade for taxonomic AI/satellite reviews.

4. **Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews** confirms its prior as the
   notorious B-grade venue: PRISMA-style literature counting plus pros/cons
   tables, with effect-size numbers (LCOE, capacity factor, energy density)
   transcribed from primary papers without normalization to common
   assumptions. Disagreements typically stated, not resolved. The headline
   sweep-without-verification pattern is overwhelmingly modal here.

**Comparison to natural science.** Three differences are sharp.

(i) *Original verification is more often built in by the authors' own
work.* In econ, the JEL/handbook author is typically a leading practitioner
asked to assemble a synthesis around their own and adjacent estimates; the
re-estimation step is nearly compulsory. In top engineering reviews
(*Annual Review of Control / BME*, *Progress in Energy & Combustion*) the
modal pattern is "the author's group re-runs / re-derives a representative
slice rather than every primary claim." Natural-science *Annual Reviews*
sit in between: reviewers more often re-curate datasets but seldom
re-estimate.

(ii) *Negative-space discipline is venue-bound.* JEL and *Annual Review*
Economics surveys treat "what we don't know" as a structural section, often
with explicit "open questions" lists. Engineering reviews are more variable:
*Annual Reviews* and *Progress in …* journals enforce it; *Renewable and
Sustainable Energy Reviews* and many IEEE application reviews substitute a
"future work" bullet list, which we score as weak negative-space treatment.

(iii) *B-grade venues exist in both clusters but differ in their failure
mode.* In econ, the failure mode of a weak survey is a literature
recap that fails to reconcile competing point estimates. In engineering, the
failure mode is bibliometric counting + technology taxonomy with no
reproduction or re-normalization. The autonomous-agent skill should treat
both as the same anti-pattern: "summarized claims with no independent check
on whether they reconcile or even use comparable units."

**Implication for the autonomous-agent survey skill.** The skill should
require, at minimum, one of: (a) author re-estimation on harmonized data, (b)
unified formal model that subsumes prior models, (c) model-meta-classification
that maps prior models to a common 2D parameter space, or (d) author re-
simulation / re-experiment that puts cited results on a single reference-
condition axis. Bibliometric counting plus pros/cons tables alone is
B-grade and should be flagged.
