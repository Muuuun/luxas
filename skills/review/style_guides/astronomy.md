# Astronomy Review Style Guide (ARA&A / ApJL mega-collaboration house voice)

## Narrative voice

Astronomy reviews split into two distinct registers. The Annual Review register—Somerville & Davé, Naab & Ostriker, Andrews, Bastian & Lardo, Özel & Freire—is pedagogical and neutral in temperature, first-person-plural at framing moments but impersonal in the body; paragraphs run long and close on a synthesis beat before the next opens. The mega-collaboration register—Planck 2018, GWTC-3, EHT Paper I—is archival and detached, organized around data provenance and methodological transparency, conclusions delivered as bullet-point checklists. Both genres share a commitment to exactness: uncertainties are quoted to the last decimal place, and the significance of a tension is never left unquantified.

## Opening conventions

Small-author ARA&A reviews open one of three ways: a field-landscape paragraph staging observational abundance before the theoretical problem (Somerville & Davé; Andrews); a direct rhetorical question decomposed into numbered sub-problems (Naab & Ostriker: "Do we understand galaxy formation?"); or a before/after historical pivot keyed to a predecessor review (Özel & Freire: "Our understanding of neutron stars has changed drastically since the Annual Reviews article of Wheeler (1966)..."). In all cases the first citation arrives within two or three sentences, and a thesis—claim about knowledge state or frank statement of what remains unsolved—appears before the end of the first paragraph. Mega-collaboration papers open with a single declarative sentence naming the instrument and its main finding, followed immediately by a roadmap sentence outlining the paper's structure. Hook paragraphs run 90–145 words.

## Section architecture

ARA&A monographs run 6–8 top-level sections, each subdivided three to five levels deep, typical section length 12–25 paragraphs. Titles favor nominal topics ("Neutron Star Mass Measurements") or gerund processes ("Simulating Dark Matter"). A concluding "Summary and Prospects" or "Outlook" section is universal, 6–10 paragraphs. Method and theory sections precede observational confrontation, even in nominally observational reviews—the measurement framework comes before the measurements. Mega-collaboration papers follow a data-pipeline order: instrument status, data quality, search methods, parameter estimation, conclusion. Both genres include a table of contents for papers exceeding ~30 pages.

## Thesis-per-section convention

ARA&A sections open with a claim about the physical phenomenon, not a citation. The claim may be a paradox, a convergence result, or an assertion of scope:

"The physical origins of the demographic behaviors that were outlined in the previous section are presumably closely related to the growth and migration of the disk solids." — Andrews (ARA&A 2020, §3)

"Dark matter builds the backbone for the formation of galaxies, which are expected to form at the centers of dark matter overdensities, so-called halos." — Vogelsberger et al. (Nat. Rev. Phys. 2020, §3)

"Finding the maximum mass of neutron stars is of particular interest in mass measurements because of its direct implications for the neutron star equation of state." — Özel & Freire (ARA&A 2016, §2.4)

Mega-collaboration sections open with a forward reference to the companion paper justifying the methodology—genre convention rather than rhetorical choice.

## Synthesis moves

**The master comparison table.** Every corpus review anchors synthesis in a table mapping the landscape: simulation suites with numerical methods and resolutions (Vogelsberger et al.), pulsar masses with measurement techniques (Özel & Freire), GW candidates with pastro scores (GWTC-3). These tables are the rhetorical center of gravity; prose sections explain table entries rather than replace them.

**The truth table or failure matrix.** Competing models evaluated against observational constraints are rendered as a grid with explicit success/failure symbols rather than discursive paragraphs (Bastian & Lardo: "A graphical summary of the comparison between predictions for the proposed models and observations"). Disagreements become public and permanent rather than softened in prose.

**Contrast pairs at the section boundary.** Naab & Ostriker exemplify the canonical split: "The current state of the art shows a good grasp of problem (A)...but with regard to the more complex problem (B)...we have only preliminary gropings toward physical understanding." This what-works vs. what-does-not structure is the dominant organizing logic in ARA&A.

**The exclusion argument.** Prominent in EHT and GW papers: each alternative hypothesis is named and eliminated by specific observable criterion ("An Einstein ring formed by gravitational lensing of a bright region in the counter-jet would require a fine-tuned alignment and a size larger than that measured in 2012 and 2009"—EHT Paper I, §8). The completeness of the exclusion list constitutes evidence for the main claim.

## Transition moves

Five verbatim bridging formulas from the corpus:

"The physical origins of the demographic behaviors...are presumably closely related to the growth and migration of the disk solids." — Andrews (§2→§3; bridge)

"Until recently, nearly all of the constraints on disk structures were derived from observations with relatively coarse spatial resolution, ≳15–20 au. As is frequently the case, improved facilities and instrumentation have precipitated a dramatic shift in the field." — Andrews (§3→§4; historical pivot)

"Ultimately, both of these problems are consequences of the predicted (high) efficiency for the radial migration of solids. The next section reconsiders an elegant solution to both problems, achieved by relaxing the standard assumption of a smooth gas disk." — Andrews (§3→§4; bridge with thesis preview)

"So far we have been describing this as an ab initio problem of physics, like the motion of a playground swing—though more complex—but of course this is not the way that the history unfolded." — Naab & Ostriker (§1; historical pivot)

"Such tests of gravity theories are not the topic of this review...Nevertheless, systems like J0737–3039 are important for NS mass measurements because they demonstrate experimentally that any pair of mass constraints chosen from q and the PK parameters will generally yield consistent NS masses." — Özel & Freire (§2; caveat)

## Citation voice

Astronomy reviews are parenthetical-dominant: authorship is rarely named in prose. Author names appear in running text to credit a landmark discovery or to mark a specific disagreement. Citation density averages 3–5 parenthetical groups per paragraph in body sections; in comparison passages it rises to 8–12, chains of 5–15 references signaling community consensus rather than individual attribution. The introduction cites at 2–3 per paragraph. Mega-collaboration papers cite at 5–7 per paragraph throughout, with named citations almost entirely absent outside the opening historical sentence.

## Anti-stacking discipline

Top ARA&A reviews open more than half their body paragraphs with a claim about the physical phenomenon, folding authorship into parenthetical clauses mid-sentence or at paragraph end:

"Cluster mass does appear to be an important parameter for GCs, playing a role in determining whether MPs are present, but also in the properties of the MPs. The first hints for this came from Carretta et al. (2010)..." — Bastian & Lardo (§2.2)

"The combined effects of growth and migration—both vertically (settling) and radially (drift)—profoundly influence the properties of disk solids (Testi et al. 2014). The simplest distillation of the key predictions..." — Andrews (§3.1)

When stacking does appear, the authorship lead is immediately followed by a contrast phrase ("More recent methods apply empirical corrections..."—Somerville & Davé, §3.2), marking the pivot from survey to analysis.

## Figure and equation roles

ARA&A reviews lead Figure 1 with a schematic or cartoon establishing the conceptual vocabulary before any data. Subsequent figures are data figures—mass-radius diagrams, spectral index distributions, simulation comparison galleries—with captions opening on what is shown, not what is inferred. Equation density varies by subfield: observational papers run 0.1–0.5 per page, simulation reviews 0.5–1.0, cosmological parameter papers 1.5–2.0. Equations are numbered and re-referenced. Boxes or sidebars (Nature Reviews tcolorbox; Annual Reviews margin notes) appear in roughly half the corpus papers, isolating definitions or worked examples without disrupting narrative flow.

## Signature moves

1. **Begin each section with a physics claim, not a citation.** The first sentence states what is true, what is unknown, or what the section will demonstrate—never "Smith et al. (2020) showed."
2. **Anchor synthesis in a table or grid.** The comparison table, truth table, or parameter catalog is the review's central evidence; prose sections comment on it rather than replace it.
3. **Name tensions explicitly with quantitative content.** Hubble tension at 2.5σ, drift timescales too short by a factor of several, mass-budget problem in globular clusters—tensions are measured and left open, not smoothed away.
4. **Use the before/after historical arc.** Cite the predecessor review, state what was unknown then, what is known now, and what the present review adds.
5. **Reserve first-person for interpretive commitment.** "We will argue," "We conclude," "We find a tension"—these appear at section openings or closings, not scattered through body paragraphs, marking the moments when authors speak as scientists rather than reporters.
