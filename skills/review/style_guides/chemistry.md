# Chemistry Review Style Guide (Chemical Reviews monograph voice)

## Narrative voice

Chemistry reviews at Chemical Reviews operate across a wide rhetorical spectrum, but the center of gravity is the archival monograph: neutral in temperature, third-person or first-plural-we, organized to serve as a durable reference rather than to argue a single thesis. The prose is efficient and cumulative — each paragraph deposits one layer of a taxonomy before the next opens — with warmth surfacing only at section boundaries or in outlook paragraphs. A minority of shorter reviews (Science, specialist Chemical Reviews essays) shift into a warmer, opinionated register that freely uses "we" to frame strategic claims and assess tradeoffs, but even these stay controlled: evaluation appears at the opening and closing of sections, not woven through every sentence. In all registers, the writing is evidence-first: a claim earns its place only when a structure, rate constant, or synthetic yield is there to anchor it.

## Opening conventions

Roughly half of the corpus opens with a historical arc tracing a field from founding experiment to present growth ("The discovery of graphene and layered transition metal oxides... ignited considerable interest" — Vittal, Chem. Rev. 2021, §1). The other half opens with a field-landscape framing: a broad statement of technical or societal importance followed by the scope of the review ("Creating a global-scale sustainable energy system... is one of the most crucial challenges facing humanity today" — Norskov et al., Science 2017, §1). Big-number statistics (TW of global energy demand, Mt of CO2) occasionally substitute for historical arcs in applied-chemistry reviews. Across all subtypes, the first citation arrives in sentence one — chemistry does not allow uncited territory even in the opening gesture. Hook paragraphs run 80–120 words, and a thesis sentence (the scope of the current review, not a finding) typically appears by the end of paragraph one or at the start of paragraph two.

## Section architecture

Long Chemical Reviews monographs (40–140 pages) carry 6–15 top-level sections, each with 4–12 subsections organized by material platform, bond type, topology label, or reaction substrate. Section titles are almost universally nominal-topic phrases ("Design Strategies," "Carbon–Carbon Bond Formation," "Properties and Applications") rather than questions or full declarative statements — the title announces a category, not a conclusion. A Summary and Outlook section, typically 3–6 paragraphs, closes every monograph and returns to field-level assessment. Shorter specialist reviews (15–30 pages) compress to 4–6 sections with less subsectioning but preserve the outlook close. Theory, experiment, and application are integrated within sections rather than split into separate blocks; a reaction mechanism, the experimental conditions that activate it, and at least one natural-product or material target appear in the same paragraph sequence.

## Thesis-per-section convention

Sections open more often with orientation than with a bold claim. The orienting sentence announces what category is being entered and may gesture toward its organizing principle, without asserting a finding:

"A 2D polymeric structure may be flat, undulated, curled, containing single layer, bilayer, or even more than two but finite layers." — Vittal, Chem. Rev. 2021, §2

"Cyclizations of nitrogen-centered radicals have long played a role in the synthesis of alkaloids and other nitrogenous natural products." — Pitre & Overman, Chem. Rev. 2021, §3

"The well-defined 2D MOF structures with ordered channels, pores, and functional groups enable a broad range of physicochemical properties and applications." — Vittal, Chem. Rev. 2021, §5

When a section does lead with a structural claim, it is almost always a quantitative fact or a mechanistic constraint ("SAAs are a type of single-site catalyst generally comprised of reactive dopants atomically isolated in a less reactive metal host" — Sykes & Friend, Chem. Rev. 2020, §2), not an evaluative judgment. Evaluative judgments appear at section closes.

## Synthesis moves

**Topology-label taxonomy.** Monographs covering porous materials (COFs, MOFs) assign each compound a RCSR or IUPAC topology code (sql, hxl, hcb, kgm) and classify structures by node connectivity and grid geometry. This converts hundreds of diverse compounds into a unified geometric grammar and allows encyclopedic survey without narrating each compound individually. Exemplified in Vittal (Chem. Rev. 2021, §2–§5) and Jiang (Chem. Rev. 2020, §2–§4).

**Volcano-plot unification.** In electrocatalysis reviews, a single descriptor — typically ΔG of a key surface intermediate — is plotted against experimental activity for all available catalyst families, producing a volcano curve that ranks every material on one axis. The curve becomes the review's spine: all subsequent discussion is positioned relative to the volcano's apex. Exemplified in Norskov et al. (Science 2017, §2–§3).

**Bond-type retrosynthetic axis.** Synthesis reviews organize by the bond that is formed or broken in the key photocatalytic step (C–C intermolecular, C–C cyclization, C–N, C–C fragmentation), not by catalyst or substrate class. Each section opens with a retrosynthetic disconnection of the target molecule before presenting the photocatalytic route. Exemplified in Pitre & Overman (Chem. Rev. 2021, §2–§4).

**Comparison with alternatives.** Nearly every monograph inserts an explicit contrast between the reviewed approach and classical alternatives: top-down vs. bottom-up exfoliation; photoredox conditions vs. tin-hydride radical methods; SAA catalysts vs. conventional nanoparticle heterogeneous catalysts. The contrast motivates the review's framing without requiring a polemical tone.

**Running natural-product example.** Synthesis and photocatalysis reviews anchor mechanistic discussion in a named target (strychnos alkaloids, zephycarinatine D, (+)-GB22) that recurs across multiple paragraphs, allowing the reader to trace a retrosynthetic logic from initial disconnection through photocatalytic key step to completed target without losing narrative thread.

## Transition moves

Chemistry reviews move between sections with a short orienting bridge sentence that restates the incoming section's organizing category before enumerating examples. Verbatim examples:

"A number of 2D sheets are made from different types of 1D CPs by interdigitation, interpenetration, or interlocking." — Vittal, §2→§3, bridge function.

"In 2014, Stephenson and co-workers used a photoredox-catalyzed C–C bond fragmentation approach in the semisynthesis of indole alkaloids." — Pitre & Overman, §3→§4, historical pivot.

"We begin by presenting theoretical results with a focus on understanding catalytic trends using a descriptor-based approach." — Norskov et al., intro→§HER, scope statement.

"There is hope that the design strategies discussed here for 2D MOFs provide access for making new MOFs with desired properties." — Vittal, §6→§7, zoom-out.

"While these volcanoes provide insight into the optimum catalyst for a given class of catalyst materials, there are additional factors not present in the simple descriptor-based model that are needed to quantitatively determine absolute reaction rates." — Norskov et al., mid-§HER, caveat.

## Citation voice

Parenthetical citation dominates throughout the corpus. Reference numbers or author-year tags appear at the end of the sentence or clause, after the claim; the claim itself is grammatically independent of the citation. Average citation density runs 4–6 references per paragraph in body sections, dropping to 2–3 in outlook paragraphs. Introductions land citations at high density from sentence one.

Named-group attribution in prose ("Sykes and co-workers reported," "Biradha and Fujita reviewed") is reserved for genuine historical pivots — a first synthesis, a founding characterization technique, a concept introduction — or, in synthesis reviews where community mapping is a primary function, appears as the grammatical subject of the paragraph's opening sentence at roughly 40–65% frequency. In electrocatalysis and materials reviews, authorship-in-prose is sparing (15–25% of paragraph openers); the field trusts parenthetical numbers to carry attribution weight and keeps claim sentences clean. Science-venue pieces maximize this discipline.

## Anti-stacking discipline

The strongest Chemical Reviews monographs avoid "X et al. did Y, found Z" stacking by leading with a phenomenon-level claim and weaving authorship into the predicate or parenthetical:

"Platinum sits very near to the top of the hydrogen volcano with an almost thermo-neutral ΔGH and is well-known as the best-performing catalyst for the HER, requiring negligible overpotentials to achieve high reaction rates in acidic solutions." — Norskov et al.

"The aggregation energy is a thermodynamic value based on the relative stability of the isolated dopant atoms vs dopant clusters." — Sykes & Friend.

"By using visible-light photocatalysis, it is now possible to generate carbon radicals directly from common functional groups, avoiding extra steps to introduce 'radical-generating' derivatives." — Pitre & Overman.

A stacked opening looks like: "Hardie et al. employed a hexapodal ligand hexakis(isonicotinoyl)cyclotricatechylene (hnctc) in the assembly of [Re3(hnctc)2Br3(CO)3]." This pattern (55% of paragraph openers in Vittal) is common in encyclopedic taxonomy sections where example-by-example enumeration is the goal and claim-density is deliberately low. Recognizing which mode a section is in — argument-driven versus catalog — is the key to appropriate imitation.

## Figure + equation roles

Fig. 1 across the corpus is almost universally an overview schematic: a topology diagram, reaction pathway map, or volcano curve that encodes the entire review's organizational logic in a single panel. Subsequent figures serve one of four roles: taxonomy panels (periodic tables with highlighted metals, grid-shape catalogs, bond-type trees), reaction-scheme sequences (numbered schemes tracing a synthetic route with reagents and yields inline), phase diagrams or volcano plots revisited at higher resolution, and representative structural images (crystal packing renders, STM micrographs, TEM panels). Numbered reaction schemes predominate in synthesis reviews and function as the figure equivalent of worked examples. Equation density varies sharply by subfield: electrocatalysis carries ~1 equation per page (Norskov et al.); photocatalysis and MOF structural reviews carry essentially none (<0.05 per page). Tables appear selectively in methodological sections (synthesis method comparison tables, characterization technique comparison). No corpus paper uses numbered boxes or sidebars.

## Signature moves

1. **Topology code as organizing spine.** Every 2D or 3D framework structure is assigned its RCSR topology label before any other property is stated; the label then serves as the primary index key for cross-referencing hundreds of compounds without re-narrating each one.

2. **Volcano at the center.** Electrocatalysis and heterogeneous catalysis reviews commit to one descriptor volcano per reaction class as the quantitative backbone; all catalyst discussion is an annotation on this backbone, and every new material is positioned relative to the apex.

3. **Named-group sentence subject as community credit.** Synthesis and materials-characterization reviews (SAA, 2D MOF, photoredox synthesis) place research-group names as grammatical subjects of key attribution sentences — not to stack, but to perform the community-mapping function that is part of the Chemical Reviews monograph's institutional role.

4. **Crystallographic parameter as mechanistic evidence.** Specific interatomic distances (olefin separation ≤4.2 Å for Schmidt's rule, cavity dimensions in Å, M–M distances in SAAs) appear as argument within sentences, turning crystallographic data into narrative proof rather than supplementary footnote. This move is distinctive to structure-driven subfields and absent in purely computational reviews.

5. **Outlook as distillation, not disclaimer.** The closing outlook section extracts one or two transferable design rules from the body — "radical reactions occur from the less-hindered face of the carbon radical"; "no single characterization technique suffices to verify atomic dispersion" — framed as portable lessons rather than generic calls for more research. The best closing paragraphs read as the review's thesis, finally stated after the evidence has accumulated.
