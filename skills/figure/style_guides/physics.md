# Physics Figure Style Guide (Nature house voice)

## Visual voice
Physics figures in Nature read clinical, precise, and quietly confident — instruments speaking rather than designers speaking. Palettes skew mixed-leaning-cool; saturation is vivid but disciplined; typography refuses decoration. Backgrounds are pure white, gridlines absent in essentially every panel, and color spent sparingly and always semantically. When warmth appears it is surgical — a single salmon or crimson trace against a navy field to name a pump laser, a positive Hall sector, a theoretical fit. The feel is a laboratory notebook by a careful draftsman: tight margins, bold panel letters, dense but legible annotation, no flourish that isn't doing quantitative work.

## Figure 1 schematic conventions
About three quarters of physics papers lead with a hybrid Figure 1 fusing a polished-vector schematic with adjacent data or device panels. The schematic anchors the concept — a Bloch/Poincaré sphere, a layered hBN/graphene/SiO₂ device stack, a hexagonal Brillouin zone with Berry-curvature arrows, a Hall-bar micrograph, an optics block diagram, a lattice with colored spins, or a mechanical metaphor (gears, pendulums) mapped onto the physics. Craft is polished-vector with hard edges, flat fills, and at most a whisper of matte shading to imply 3D on spheres, gears, or device slabs; drop shadows and glows are forbidden. Strokes sit ~0.75–1 pt; bold-triangle arrowheads carry signal flow or force vectors, hairline arrows with filled heads carry molecule trajectories or k-vectors. Color is restrained to three or four hues plus black — a warm accent (red/salmon) naming the "problem" or pumped element, a cool accent (steel blue or navy) naming the neutral/signal path, black for structural lines. Panels sit in grid-tight groupings labeled **a b c** in bold lowercase sans-serif at top-left, reading left-to-right then top-down, one scale bar per micrograph. Place the schematic and its measurement consequence (dispersion, map, spectrum) on adjacent panels so the visual argument chain is immediate.

## Palette
Categorical traces reach first for Matplotlib `tab10` under strict semantic assignment: `#1f77b4` steel blue, `#d62728` crimson, `#2ca02c` forest green, `#ff7f0e` orange, `#9467bd` purple, plus `#000000` as a full-strength categorical color. A cooler "instrument" register recurs in optics and condensed-matter work — navy `#1a2a3a`, muted salmon `#c0504d`, steel blue `#4a7ebb`, cyan-teal `#5bb8c4`. Three working combinations from the corpus: (1) **navy + salmon** for main-data vs. reference/unsynchronized state; (2) **steel blue + crimson + forest green** for three-filter or three-parameter encodings, held across light curves, CI bands, and corner-plot marginals; (3) **deep periwinkle `#3a4a8a` + magenta `#ff00cc` + yellow `#ffee00`** for paired amplitude/phase or real-space/Fourier maps.

## Color conventions
Color is semantic, never ornamental. Once a hue is bound to a physical identity — a band, a filter, a spin state, a pump laser, the "this-work" dataset — it carries that identity through every panel. Line and marker colors match exactly within a series. Grey and black are reserved for theoretical models, noise floors, or raw reference. Diverging blue-white-red with ±1 normalization encodes signed quantities (Stokes, Hall sign, rate differences); perceptually uniform sequential maps (viridis/inferno/plasma) encode magnitude. Transparency is sparing and purposeful: alpha ~0.20–0.30 for confidence bands color-matched to their line; alpha ~0.15–0.25 for translucent window fills (synchronization bands, KIS regions) laid directly over data rather than called out in legends. Lines themselves essentially never get alpha.

## Markers & data encoding
The shape vocabulary is small and stable: filled circles dominate for primary "this-work" data, open circles mark experimental points against simulation/fit curves, and filled squares, upward triangles, diamonds, and stars disambiguate literature sources when datasets overlay. Sizes are 4–9 pt and do not encode magnitude. Filled markers use solid color with either no stroke or a ~0.5 pt same-color edge; open markers use a ~1 pt colored edge on white fill. Cross and plus glyphs mark special k-points, maxima, or upper-limit indicators. Color carries categorical identity within a dataset; shape carries it across datasets.

## Lines & hierarchy
Data traces run 1.0–1.5 pt, fit and model curves 1.5–2 pt, axis spines ~0.5–0.75 pt, guide lines ~0.75 pt. Dashed lines are strictly for theory, simulation, expected slopes, zero baselines, or quantile markers — never for data. Long-dash for fits, dash-dot for secondary models, short-dash for noise floors or boundaries. Joins rounded, ends butt or rounded, no white halos. Z-order: fit lines over scatter, colored categorical traces over grey reference, CI fills beneath their line.

## Typography
Sans-serif throughout — Helvetica Neue or Arial, never a serif. Panel letters ~10–11 pt **bold** at the top-left of each panel; axis titles 8–9 pt regular; tick labels 7–8 pt regular; in-panel annotations and legends 7–8 pt regular. Bold is reserved almost exclusively for the panel letter. Math is inline mathtext with italic variables, Unicode Greek (μ, δ, ω, Δ), and true sub/superscripts — no faux-italic, no plain-text substitutes. Units appear in typographic form (R⊙, ℏ, Pa). Quantitative callouts — fit parameters, time delays, magnifications — sit as a left-justified text block inside the plot area, not in a separate table.

## Composition & whitespace
Panels sit on a mosaic grid, often irregular (1×N rows, 2×N blocks, corner-plot triangles) but always grid-tight, with inter-panel gutters ~3–6 pt. Spine convention splits by panel type: all-four closed-box frames on image panels, heatmaps, k-space maps, and ARPES data; bottom-left-only L-frame axes on standard 1D line and scatter plots. Ticks are inward, major-only, 4–6 per axis. Legends live inside the plot box, flush to an empty corner, with no enclosing frame. Insets embed freely — a device micrograph inside the spectrum panel, a zoom of an avoided crossing, a Brillouin-zone key — taking independent scales. Dual x-axes (frequency + mode number; pressure + altitude; rest-frame + observer-frame days) are a recurring move bridging two coordinates in one panel.

## Error representation
Error bars are thin vertical I-bars, capped with flat horizontal whiskers, full-opacity in the series color. Model-fit confidence intervals appear as color-matched shaded bands at ~0.20–0.25 alpha beneath the solid model line. Posterior structure shows as nested filled contours with sequentially increasing alpha (typically blue-to-purple), with marginal histograms overlaid by a solid red MAP line and dashed blue quantile lines. Upper limits are downward arrows with horizontal-only error bars. If there is no uncertainty to report on a trace, nothing is drawn.

## Signature moves
1. **Semantic color locking across an entire paper** — one hue (salmon for the reference pump, navy for the main comb, red for C=+1) tracks one physical identity through schematic, spectrum, map, and statistical panels, so the reader follows an object by eye alone.
2. **Diverging blue–white–red with ±1 normalization on 2D maps**, making sign reversals (Stokes, Hall, Chern) readable at a glance without annotation.
3. **Dual x-axes** pairing a physical coordinate (Hz, Pa, observer-frame day) with a natural/modal coordinate (mode number μ, altitude km, rest-frame day).
4. **Embedded quantitative callouts** — numerical reference values printed at the right spine next to dash-dot guide lines, or parameter blocks left-justified inside the data area — so the reader never consults a caption to read a number.
5. **Panel-array narratives instead of overlays** — varying one parameter across four or five values as a row of contour panels with shared axes and a single horizontal colorbar below, letting the transformation of the landscape itself carry the argument.
