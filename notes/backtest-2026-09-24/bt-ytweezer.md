## 3. False-positive and cost side (this project)

- **M1.** Natural published anchors exist for 2 of the 6 experiments: E2 (static α = 162) and E1 (Hannaford BF, but that anchor is circular). E3 has only cross-species imaging anchors, and those run in magic traps. E4 (counting synthesis) and E5 (a literature comparison table) have **no reasonable anchor**. **Deadlock risk: high.** M1 blocks E2 on 99.1 vs 162. The successor project (`yttrium-magic-wavelengths-20260906`) shows that even a merged NIST+Kurucz SOS with continuum and core reached only 146.6, below its 150 pass line, and ended **without running the magic scan at all**. Under M1, this project would have produced no trap design, although its 14.4 mK/W lower bound is legitimately useful.
- **M2.** There are 10 declared xval legs (E1: 3, E2: 2, E3: 1, E4: 2, E5: 2). About 8 would be downgraded: the E1 circular pair, E2 harmonic vs Hessian, E3 MC vs analytic, E4 set-union and flux geometry, and E5 read-the-same-value-twice. **All of these downgrades are correct**, since none is physics-independent. The only legitimate survivor is E5's Dy g_J (LS Landé vs measured). If finish required at least one INDEPENDENT per headline, nearly every headline would fail, so this is a deadlock unless there is a disclose path.
- **M3.** It would block legitimate work because E1–E4 results.json were all hand-edited after generation. Most edits were prose fixes, tolerance changes (E4 tol 0→0.25), or the Infinity→1e30 fix. Regenerating E1 would **reintroduce 3 known bugs** (memory.md), a latent deadlock. Anchor values such as 162 a₀³ and 25.26 MHz from τ equal literature values and would need allow-listing.
- **M4.** Very low value here. The only infra failures were glm 429s, which were already retried. No skip-and-disclose occurred in this project.
- **M5.** It needs 4 extra reviews (E1–E4; E2b could be exempted as descoped). The E5 review cost was small (2 opus rounds) and Anthropic-billed, so it fit in the budget at the time: the run ended at $106 of a $135 cap. A REVISE on E3 would have forced an E3 re-run while DeepSeek credit was nearly exhausted, a budget risk but not a structural deadlock. Evidence against optimism: the one review that did happen (E5) was "satisfied" with zero findings, and the four PI reviews by opus-4-6 missed D1, D4 and D5.
- **M6.** Nothing to inject. `~/.sisyphus/memory.md` has 1 entry. The only earlier related project (Ba, 08-29) carries no lesson about SOS completeness, pole filtering, light shifts or the NIST hfs absence.

Forced tests on these would be ritual. The real bugs (D1, D4) lived in the experiment's run-level `compose.py` or in its *description*, outside what a blind tool test sees.
